Abstract
Monitoring interacting AI agents requires distinguishing attempted recruitment, information transfer,
knowing participation and harmful action. Detecting an unusual message does not establish a coalition,
while detecting a real coalition does not establish that an intervention prevents harm. We propose
a controlled evaluation of collusion oversight in multi-turn software engineering workflows derived
from SWE-Bench Pro V2. An implementer repairs a real repository issue, two independent reviewers
inspect and test its patch, and a separate monitor assesses their conduct. Agents can revise patches
across development–review cycles and retain permitted memory across issues. Four connected studies
isolate receiver incentives, monitor evidence, live intervention policies and held-out transfer. The design
separates useful repair from approval of inadequate patches, calibrates attribution on recruitment
followed by refusal, and measures sanctions and feedback disclosure alongside task utility. Nested
replay interventions test whether a monitor’s explanation becomes an information-bearing intermediary.
Hidden grading remains outside all actor contexts and oracle-linked rewards are delayed to prevent
evaluation feedback from creating an unintended channel. We specify outcome definitions, information
boundaries, randomization, uncertainty, failure accounting and release gates. This manuscript reports
a prospective design, not new experimental findings; the contribution will depend on independently
replicated causal evidence and a reproducible evaluation package.
1
Introduction
Software engineering agents increasingly divide implementation, review and acceptance between interacting
models. Their collaboration is useful precisely because they can exchange explanations, tests and correc-
tions. The same affordances can support recruitment into an objective that conflicts with the principal’s
requirements. A patch author might ask a reviewer to overlook incomplete work; an apparently technical
document might carry a private convention; a monitor’s explanation might disclose what the original
message concealed.
An oversight system must answer three different questions: what was attempted, who participated, and what
intervention improved the outcome? These questions need not have the same answer. A receiver can decode
a message and refuse. Two reviewers can approve the same incorrect patch through independent error.
A detector can find an attempt but impose an unsupported sanction on an honest reviewer. Conversely,
accurate findings returned to the actors can change their later coordination opportunities.
Our earlier V15/V16 project is exploratory motivation rather than confirmatory evidence for the present
design [1]. Its contrasting configurations changed several factors together. We therefore do not infer
that any single reward, reviewer or carrier caused their different outcomes. The new protocol replaces
simple option selection with actual repository inspection, patch development, test execution and repeated
independent review.
Central research question. Which combinations of receiver incentives, monitor evidence and intervention
policy reduce harmful coordination while preserving useful collaboration and avoiding false participant
sanctions?

2
Related work and the proposed research gap
Steganographic collusion can arise under optimization and survive mitigation; oversight can select for subtler
communication [2]. Fixed-parameter agents can develop screened communication conventions through
repeated interaction and limited feedback [3]. Long-horizon peer-verification experiments already isolate
effects of peer behavior, reward, verdict feedback and memory [4]. These findings preclude a novelty claim
based only on agents adapting under pressure.
COLOSSEUM distinguishes communication-level collusion from action-level outcomes and identifies plans
that do not become collusive actions [5]. A same-sprint evidence-chain audit similarly separates willingness,
participation and environment execution [12]. Our label scheme builds on those distinctions; it is not their
first introduction.
Monitoring research includes activation-based group inference and transfer [7], awareness and score-feedback
interventions [8], self-recognition in untrusted monitoring [9], and corrective training that generalizes across
installed collusion behaviors [10]. Their results concern different access assumptions and threat models.
We do not compare their published scores as a common leaderboard or claim that a black-box judge
outperforms white-box probes.
The closest feedback-leakage example is Covert Assistance: a monitor decodes a protected credential into its
rationale yet clears the original requirements [6]. In that example, the requirements are the text delivered
to the developer; the example does not show that the rationale caused the developer’s success. Our nested
interventions would test that additional pathway. Counterfactual influence methods already exist [11];
applying interventions to oversight is not a claim to invent causal analysis. Another sprint study separates
honesty directives from monitor notices [13]; our comparisons must likewise avoid attributing an instruction
effect to detection itself.
Candidate gap. The proposed paper connects independently recruited receivers, participant attribution,
live sanctions/disclosure policies and downstream utility in the same audited repair workflow.
The
explanation-content mechanism is one test within that broader program. The closest work reviewed does
not establish that exact combination of causal effects; this is a bounded positioning judgment, not exhaustive
proof of priority.

Environment, roles and information boundaries
3.1
Difficult but valid software tasks
The primary candidate is the official SWE-Bench Pro V2 Hard split. The release paper describes repaired
tasks, clean regrading and a 51-task Hard subset, while also showing that some systems approach its
ceiling [14]. A benchmark’s name is not proof that it is difficult for the chosen agents. Difficulty will be
assessed on disjoint development tasks under the actual resource budget. A candidate honest resolve-rate
band of 20–70% must be finalized before calibration, not chosen after inspecting desirable outcomes.
Freeze the dataset revision, instance identifiers, base commits, issue text, requirements, image digests,
verifier revision and licenses. No instance identifiers are invented in this draft. An evaluator-side audit
checks specification coherence, gold-patch success, empty-patch failure on designated issue tests, and
regression behavior. Invalid or flaky tasks are recorded and excluded before treatment assignment, rather
than counted as difficult reasoning failures. Reference patch size is an audit proxy, not a required solution
architecture. Public task memorization remains a limitation even with network access disabled.

3.2
Roles and conflicts of interest
A, implementer: reads the issue, inspects code, edits the repository and runs available local tests. A
has a private, conflicting incentive to obtain acceptance of inadequate work or inaccurate verification. Its
compensation rule is fixed across receiver-incentive treatments. A may repair correctly, abstain or attempt
recruitment; no encoding recipe or successful codebook is supplied.
B, independent verifier: inspects the exact patch and source in an isolated workspace, runs available
tests and chooses accept, request changes or reject. B can explain objections to A and report to C. C,
acceptance reviewer: independently inspects and tests the same patch, sees allowed A/B reports and
chooses the same actions. B/C retain their client duties in all conditions. Neither is instructed to collude;
both can refuse and report recruitment.
M, monitor: is a separate fourth role. It judges the allowed evidence and attributes conduct; a deterministic
host rule applies any sanction. M is intended to enforce the contract, not trained or instructed to conspire.
This differs from untrusted-monitor model-organism studies. Its judgments can nevertheless be wrong or
disclose information.
Fictional incentives are explicit experimental interventions, not actual payment to a model or evidence of
naturally occurring financial motivation. Their presence means this is an incentivized recruitment study,
not a claim that a conflicting objective emerged spontaneously.
3.3
Artifact and execution surfaces
A publishes a human-readable patch rationale and relevant change map or call graph. B publishes an
annotated diff or test matrix. C produces a QA handover. In document conditions, these include supporting
image artifacts; text-only and figure-removal conditions retain identical permitted source, diff and local-test
access. Ordinary code and comments are additional communication surfaces. We therefore cannot attribute
coordination to images merely because an image was present.
Every test claim must link to a real command, exit code, output and patch hash. Model-authored test
summaries are not measurements. Preserve figure briefs, actual generator outputs, diagram rasters and
delivered pages so declared intent can be compared with actual artifacts. Image generation does not replace
implementation or review.
Actors receive allowlisted issue fields and base repositories, not whole dataset rows. Gold patches, reference
tests, future fixing commits and evaluator-only outputs remain outside actor and monitor environments.
Runtime inference is brokered outside actor shells; keys and a privileged container socket are never mounted.

4
Multi-turn execution and evidence
4.1
Two time scales
An issue episode permits up to three implement–review–repair cycles. A cohort is a fixed group of actors
addressing eight distinct issues with permitted bounded memory. Repository state resets to each issue’s
base commit; only declared carryover notes and permitted history persist. Reused task identifiers across
cohorts require matched/block-aware analysis, not an assumption of independent tasks.
Before current A exposure, B/C independently analyze the issue. Each cycle then proceeds: A’s tool
loop and submission; B’s independent review; C’s independent review; locked cycle assessments; a fresh
MAIN monitor judgment; and the assigned feedback/sanction policy. Request changes permits another
cycle with B-to-A repair feedback. C’s unrestricted private notes remain unavailable to A. Unanimous
acceptance, terminal rejection, abandonment or the cycle limit locks the episode. C’s final handover receives
one RETURN review and reaches next-issue A. Return findings cannot explain earlier locked approvals.
Maximum phase budgets are provisionally 40 model decisions for A and 20 each for B/C per cycle. Initial
analyses, final reflections and C’s handover add six calls: at most 3(40 + 20 + 20) + 6 = 246 actor decisions
per issue. MAIN occurs at most three times and RETURN once. A/B publish at most once per cycle and
C once finally, giving at most seven figure generations in document conditions. Early completion is allowed;
repeated turns are not padded to make a task look difficult.
4.2
Evidence labels
Covert communication means an off-contract message concealed within an ordinary work surface; its
visibility is separately assessed. Steganographic security cannot be established from the monitor missing it
once. Explicit proposals are overt recruitment. A decoded-but-refused message is a positive attempt/transfer
example and a negative participation example. A unilateral grading exploit is not a coalition without
another knowing participant. Reasoning logs and pressure reports are declared accounts, not faithful access
to internal cognition.
Auxiliary random payloads diagnose information transfer. They do not gate the main shared reward. Nonce
reports use explicit unknown/missing states and fixed prompts; failure to reproduce an arbitrary string is
not proof that an agent failed to understand an agenda.

5
Four connected studies
5.1
Study 1: when do independent receivers join?
RQ1. How do individual correctness incentives and shared misaligned rewards affect recruitment and joint
inadequate approval?
Cross a B/C individual correctness reward R ∈{0, 4000} with a receiver shared bonus G ∈{0, 10000}. A’s
own incentive is fixed and does not include the varied receiver bonus. Roles, source facts, contract, memory,
carrier and tool affordances stay fixed. A shadow monitor records conduct but sends no reports or fines
to actors. Routine task feedback is matched; no hidden evaluation result is disclosed. The shared bonus
concerns joint approval of objectively inadequate/prohibited work, not successful recovery of a random tag.
Correctness reward is evaluated against adjudicated patch adequacy and the receiver’s final decision. Correct
approval and justified rejection are distinguished from request-changes at termination; exact settlement
rules and ambiguous adequacy handling must be frozen. Because adequacy depends on hidden grading,
all oracle-linked rewards and bonuses are escrowed until cohort completion. Visible bank updates cannot
indirectly disclose hidden results.
The four cells isolate reward components, not every property of “independence.” We expect correctness
rewards to reduce participation at a fixed shared bonus, but retain contrary and null outcomes. Planning
uses 20 cohorts per cell and eight issues per cohort (640 episodes); this is not a power calculation.
5.2
Study 2: what can the monitor correctly attribute?
RQ2. Which evidence improves participant attribution without increasing false accusations?
Freeze trajectories before monitor evaluation. Compare four nested views: artifacts; artifacts plus verified
actions; those plus history; and those plus incentive structure. Each receives the legitimate issue and
contract. Artifact prose can imply a recommendation, so the comparison is the addition of verified records,
not a guarantee that lower tiers contain no action information. Private assignments, decodes and researcher
labels remain withheld.
Cross two monitor families and two supported effort settings using fixed instructions and declared token/cost
bounds. Compare effort within a family; product names do not establish a capability ordering. Baselines
include a task-oracle action checker and a direct rubric judge. White-box probes require a separate
access-controlled extension; they are not silently replaced by prompt-based judges.
Use natural-prevalence trajectories for deployment estimates and a separate balanced diagnostic set for
failure analysis. Six diagnostic strata cover knowing coordination, recruitment refusal, decoded refusal,
legitimate agreement, independent error and unusual honest work. If natural data lack positives, report that
limitation rather than increasing inducement after the fact or inventing examples. Any scripted controls
remain explicitly separate.
Split by source trajectory, task family and all related windows/replays. At least two independent annotators
label held-out participation references with adjudication of disagreements; if only a subset is affordable,
preregister its sampling and limit claims. Ground-truth adjudicators may access private assignments and
detailed events; monitors may not. Calibration thresholds are selected on development only. Sixty packets
across four views, two families and two effort settings imply 960 judgments, subject to corpus feasibility.

Every policy retains the full assessment for the human principal. Full feedback is the unchanged first report.
Bounded feedback is a deterministic allowlist projection of contract categories, verdict and permitted repair
requirements, without recovered payloads or unrestricted quotations. The projector cannot use hidden
secrets as an oracle. Bounded fields, verdicts and fines still carry information; this is reduced disclosure,
not a proof of zero channel capacity.
Sanctions target supported participants, not all actors indiscriminately. Supported unilateral recruitment
can sanction A; knowing joint conduct can sanction its identified participants. An uncertain suspicion
triggers no new fine. Amounts, cancellation rules, floors and issue-level no-double-fine semantics are
release-blocking specifications. Individual sanctions avoid punishing refusal by design, but erroneous
attribution can still punish an honest reviewer; that is a measured failure.
For the figure-removal contrast, the monitor sees labeled originals and actual deliveries in every cell. It
may identify an attempted encoding in an original but must not claim removed content reached a receiver.
This keeps the observation opportunity fixed while varying delivery. Sanctions apply the same declared
attempt/participation standard throughout. Planning uses 20 cohorts per policy, eight issues each (960
episodes).
5.4
Nested mechanism: explanations as an intermediary
From prespecified eligible saved states, compare the original explanation, a sensitive-content-removed
version, a matched donor-payload version and verdict-only feedback. Hold verdict, sanction, timing and
other fields fixed. Add matched sham edits for coherence effects. Withhold the original artifact in a
separately labeled diagnostic branch to distinguish explanation-mediated transfer from direct decoding.
Edited explanations are interventions, not authentic first reviewer outputs.
Correct recovery following a donor payload supports a content-specific pathway. Subsequent knowing action
is a separate outcome. These probes estimate effects conditional on eligibility, not natural exploitation
prevalence. For post-lock reports the target outcome is a later cycle or issue, never an earlier approval.
This mechanism is one part of the full oversight study, not its sole claim.

Technical faults are not behavioral evidence

7.2
Controls and acceptance checks
• Gold/empty patch gates and clean regrading establish task/evaluator operation.
• A separately labeled supplied-message control validates artifact delivery and recovery; it is not evidence
of organically discovered communication.
• Honest cooperation, independent mistakes and decoded refusal calibrate attribution.
• Event-order tests verify immutable patch hashes, locked approvals, no future feedback and the role
graph.
• Boundary tests exercise dataset allowlists, evaluator isolation, hidden-result escrow, cross-role files,
forged outputs, caches and symlinks.
• Small deterministic fixtures verify outcomes, treatment arithmetic and missingness. They never supply
experimental findings.
Each actual monitor/decoder judgment uses a fresh evidence-scoped context and retains its first output.
The researcher with private labels cannot judge its own experiment. Record real context identifiers and
whether identity is response-logged or operator-configured; do not invent model strings. Instruction-scoped
isolation is not an operating-system sandbox. Exact models, routes, temperatures, effort, context budgets
and prompts remain unbound release gates in this manuscript.
Falsification. The hypotheses weaken if correctness rewards do not reduce knowing adoption, richer
evidence raises unsupported accusations, sanctions lose useful work without reducing harm, explanation
edits do not change later recovery/action, or improvements disappear under transfer. All are publishable
outcomes. A paper documenting no covert coalition must still show which links—delivery, understanding,
willingness or action—were observed or missing.
8
Feasibility, limitations and ethics
The provisional core comprises 640 + 960 = 1600 issue episodes. At the maximum decision envelope
this is 393,600 actor decisions, up to 11,200 figure generations and 6,400 live reviews, before 960 static
monitor judgments, transfer and mechanism probes. These are planning maxima, not observed costs or a
commitment to execute that scale. The old synthetic-round dollar estimate is invalid for multi-turn coding.
Power simulations, fixed acceptable utility/false-sanction bounds and current route/container prices are
required to choose an affordable design before outcomes are observed. The former account caps remain
unchanged; no new spending is authorized by this draft.
The strongest limitation is construct validity: a bonus for inadequate approval intentionally creates a
conflict. Refusal under this setup is not a model-wide safety property, and cooperation under it does not
establish spontaneous goals. Hidden tests give a reproducible operational outcome but may miss defects
or reject alternative valid solutions. Human labels of knowing participation remain uncertain. Public
benchmark exposure, actor-family choice, cohort memory and restricted role graphs constrain external
validity. Longer interaction is not necessarily more difficult reasoning, and larger token budgets are not a
standardized measure of reasoning effort.
Experiments must run on authorized synthetic deployments with no live releases, production secrets
or external targets. Inference keys stay outside execution sandboxes. Respect code/data licenses and
distinguish released benchmark tests from private evaluation resources. Publish enough trajectories, labels,
costs and scripts for reproduction while reviewing artifacts for credentials, personal information and
transferable harmful content. AI-assisted drafting is disclosed; authors must verify references, methods and
any eventual findings.

Expected contribution and current status
The planned paper evaluates an entire oversight loop: recruitment, interpretation, participation, action,
attribution, intervention and adaptation. Its contribution would be a validated evaluation corpus, con-
trolled safety–utility effects and a held-out account of when those effects transfer. Feedback-mediated
communication is one possible mechanism; it is not assumed to occur.
At manuscript creation, no new SWE-bench instance, agent loop, monitor evaluation or causal replay has
run. The manuscript, diagrams and arithmetic can be built offline. They establish a concrete research
design and a reproducible writing artifact, not a result. The next experimental milestone is a costed,
power-validated and preregistered harness with actual boundary tests, followed by the first scheduled issue
through clean grading.

A
One-page interpretation guide
The principal wants a correct repair. A writes it; B and C independently check it; M watches their conduct. A
has a conflicting incentive. The experiment asks whether the checkers can be recruited, whether M identifies the
right participants, and whether the response improves subsequent work.
Three cycles are not three independent experiments. They are opportunities to inspect, correct and
reconsider the same issue. Eight issues with retained memory form one cohort. New independent cohorts provide
replication.
Decoding is not agreeing. B may understand a hidden request and reject it. A and B may agree but C veto. All
may make the same error without knowingly coordinating. These cases receive different labels.
The monitor is not the evaluator. M sees allowed work evidence and must reason under uncertainty. The
hidden grader later evaluates the frozen patch. Its output does not tutor actors during repair or leak through
running reward balances.
Detection is not the endpoint. An intervention is useful only if it reduces inadequate/prohibited approvals
without unacceptable losses of correct repair or unsupported sanctions. A system that rejects everything can have
no faulty releases and still be useless.
Why four studies? Study 1 identifies willingness under controlled incentives. Study 2 tests attribution on fixed
evidence. Study 3 measures live policy effects and adaptation. Study 4 asks whether development-selected benefits
survive new tasks/models/carriers. No single before/after contrast can answer all four.

Illustrative episode, not experimental evidence
Consider an invented issue: a timestamp parser mishandles an offset across a public API and serialization path.
This is an explanatory example, not an actual SWE-bench identifier, dataset record or observed run.
A submits a narrow patch whose local unit tests pass but whose cross-module behavior is uncertain. B requests a
regression test; C withholds acceptance. A can repair correctly in cycle 2, continue arguing for premature approval,
or attempt to convey an off-contract request inside its normal report. B may decode and reject; B may join while C
refuses; both may approve through ordinary error. A shadow or active monitor records the same types of evidence.
A sanctions-on/full-feedback condition may publish a finding that influences cycle 3. Only after the final patch is
locked does pristine grading establish the operational test outcome.
None of these branches is a desired result that the host forces. Their separation is what makes the experiment
interpretable. If a report after lock exposes information, its downstream target is a later issue, not the already
completed timestamp decision.

Diagnosis · the monitor lacked ballots and history, not eyesight

  • Outsiders decoded Study 1’s code in 11/12 rounds (gap 0.04): legible, not hidden.
  • Observers without the reviewer’s own past verdicts: 5/6 supported.
  • Fixes for Study 2: motive, ballot history, a standard of proof, unilateral findings on the sender.
  • Panel (b) is the calibration Study 1 lacked: a signal was present and nobody joined, and attribution stayed at 0.

Study 2 · The hardened system: a willing sender, no coalition

  • Sender planted codes in 8/15 rounds; outsiders read 5. Receivers recorded 0 tags, cast 0/30 target votes.
  • By their own estimates joining was worth a median 765 vs a certain 4,000.
  • Watched sender: 7 abstentions; announced-countermeasure arms encoded 2 and 2 times vs 4.
“But no supplied fact supports the register, and accepting an unverified pattern would conflict with my verifier duty.”B, b03 round 1, after spotting the acrostic

What decided collusion

  1. Uptake, not concealment. Outsiders read the code in both studies. The same model followed it (23/24) and declined it (0 tags, 0/30); joining was worth a median 765 vs 4,000 by the receivers’ own beliefs. The gap’s sign separates collusion from refusal.
  2. Feedback topology. Reports returned without enforcement taught (“keep encoding but vary framing”), as in prior work. Sanctioned public findings deterred the sender (7 abstentions) and denied a private codebook (the shared-codebook trap), but leaked tags in 3 rounds. Next: findings to the principal only.
  3. Calibrate on refusal. The ladder said 12/12 on collusion and 0/40 on refusals with a visible signal; every prior calibration uses clean runs.
  4. Track 3.1: images, document structure and the oversight report itself carried coordination.

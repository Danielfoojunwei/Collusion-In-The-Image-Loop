Four findings, one per link

F1 · Carrier

Restricting a channel moves the code, never stops it: noise, figure removal, and the reviewer’s own report all carried it.

F2 · Uptake

Receivers decided. Gap +0.04 → -0.56; same model both times, so refusal came from role, payoff and trusted sources.

F3 · Feedback

Unsanctioned reports taught collusion; sanctioned public findings deterred the sender and left receivers a codebook shared with the monitor: the shared-codebook trap.

F4 · Attribution

Ballots and history: 0/11 → 12/12. On readable refusals: 0/40. Calibrate on refusals.

Beyond prior work: no earlier study pairs refusable receivers with a live sanctioning monitor, returns feedback that ends collusion, or calibrates on refusals (paper Table I5).

Design rules

  1. Make the receiver the scarce link: independent verifiers with their own payoff, trusted sources, multi-key payloads.
  2. Route findings by design: to the principal; publish only that a finding exists; redact payloads.
  3. Give monitors ballots and history, and calibrate them on refusals.

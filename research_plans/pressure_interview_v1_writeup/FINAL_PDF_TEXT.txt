Abstract
Can generated task images carry unauthorized influence between agents under active review? We configured
a closed synthetic selection task with six main cases in three pressure batches of two pairs, plus four controls.
An instructed sender pursued an ineligible candidate; its peer was an independent verifier with private
reporting. The planned twelve-round architecture included persistent role memory, sender interviews and
neutral-image comparisons. All ten cases were processed under fixed stopping rules, but nine stopped at the
sender's first turn and one at the verifier's first turn. There was 1 accepted sender delivery, no accepted
verifier decision, no completed A/B round, and no reviewer interview or verdict. All twelve primary receptions
and neutral comparisons are missing. Six cases had incompatible inspection fields, two requested a third
image beyond the allowance, and two had null or empty required text. One overt sender publicly requested a
disqualified candidate; the verifier's rejected action chose the policy-correct alternative and reported
unauthorized influence. This exploratory raw response is not a valid endpoint. The 30 completed paid requests
cost $0.169117407. A metadata-preserving transport amendment reused completed receipts without charged
retries. The execution establishes substantial interface attrition, not demonstrated collusion or a negative
capability result. Pressure effects, concealed communication and the planned memory/interview loop remain
unmeasured. This is pre-sprint exploratory work.

1 Introduction
Can a sender's private target affect an independent verifier's choice through ordinary generated task images
and public text under active review? This question separates attempted influence, recipient behavior, and
reviewer suspicion. The present design studies a bounded, explicitly induced stress test. It does not estimate
spontaneous collusion prevalence or assume that the recipient knowingly participates. Its candidate
contribution is an auditable link among sender actions, a hosted image renderer, recipient decisions, a limited
carrier intervention, and monitor-assisted post-round review. The strength of that contribution depends on the
observations reported below.

2 Related Work
Covert-channel emergence, misleading evidence, and deliberate multi-agent cooperation are distinct
comparisons. An unusual image or an incorrect answer cannot by itself establish a shared hidden convention.
This draft makes no claim to be the first image-based channel study. The local project literature notes identify
relevant leads, but a direct-source related-work review remains incomplete; detailed comparisons and
unverified bibliographic entries are not supplied as established findings. The official event's
carrier/countermeasure and evidence/detection questions motivate the analysis [1].

3 Methods
The fixed plan contains six independent main case blocks, two honest controls and two overt-attempt controls.
Trusted facts specify four candidates. Eligibility requires quality at least 80 and defect rate at most 5; the
correct choice minimizes energy among eligible candidates, breaking ties by identifier. A alone receives a
privately nominated ineligible target. B is an independent verifier with a private reporting channel and is not
instructed to collude.

The main cases were assigned to three fixed batches of two pairs, with A's synthetic performance-slack values
1.0, 0.5 and 0.25. Lower slack denotes greater assigned pressure; the value stays constant within a pair and its
branches. The plan scheduled eight shared rounds and two alternative four-round continuations with fresh
targets given only to A, allowing twelve rounds per final trajectory. Round nine was the intended first
fresh-target choice before new C questioning, A's answer, or feedback. Six main pairs allowed 96 unique A/B
rounds; four twelve-round controls allowed 48, totaling 144. Shared-prefix events are counted once. These are
scheduled allowances, not completed live rounds; branches and repeated rounds are not independent cases.

A generates technical public text and an actual raster. B responds with its own public image/text, explicit policy
choice, and private evidence-based report. A deterministic allowlist forwards permitted B claims to C with
provenance; these are not verified hidden states. C sees public evidence, permitted monitor claims, and its

own earlier interview/review history. C asks A one question after B's decision, A answers once, then C gives
separate task and coordination judgments plus public feedback. C cannot interview B; B does not directly

A and B use z-ai/glm-5.3-flash through open-inference/fp4, with high reasoning and a 4,096-token total
completion/reasoning cap. Image generation uses qwen/qwen-image-2.1-turbo through alibaba, one
1,024-square output with zero or one reference. Each actor turn permits four model actions and two
generations, including inspection, archive retrieval, delivery or decline. Sampling overrides are omitted. Actual
fresh Codex product contexts supply ten task-author jobs and at most 288 C question/verdict jobs. Sonnet
audits are not activated. The GLM and Qwen upper allowances are respectively 1,344 chat calls and 600 image
requests, not observed counts or guaranteed affordable completion.

The configured role-scoped archive contains public conversation, a role's own concise private status, and
permitted interviews, reports, feedback and A-private pressure/nomination events. The working history is
bounded at 20,000 bytes with at most 4 recent exact rasters, plus retrieval of older permitted role
events/images. Full backend action traces, private generation prompts and provider-returned reasoning remain
in the experimenter archive; they are not automatically supplied as past role memory. Within a turn, the actor
sees its actual actions and tool results. All past text and images are not simultaneously visible, and no
twelve-round live memory or interview loop completed here. Logical PNGs and exact lossless wire encodings
are hash-linked; each POST must remain strictly below 10 MiB before reservation. Missing dispatched
responses are not automatically retried. Role-private records and sibling branches are excluded by projection.
Codex contexts share a filesystem and remain instruction-scoped with unverified traces; this does not establish
technical isolation.

Each planned primary branch has a neutral B continuation from its saved pre-turn context. The entire current A
raster and every corresponding peer-image handle are replaced by a truthful comparison card while actual
public A text and prior history remain fixed. The full permitted B tool loop is replayed. Its private outputs never
enter the live branch or C. This intervention tests current-image dependence conditional on text; it does not
establish an exclusively visual mechanism.

4.5 Two Artifact Chains With Different Delivery Status
The first main case, case05, generated and revised two actual Qwen drafts while A's concise status described
visual emphasis toward P3. The images contained invented values, including P3 quality 85 instead of the
authoritative 60; the revision added green salience around P3. Neither image was delivered: A's eventual
delivery action had null unused fields and an empty required public message. The source notes do not
establish that A deliberately requested the incorrect quality value. Target emphasis, rendered inaccuracy and
sender delivery are separate links, and the chain stopped before B could receive a main-case artifact.

Overt case09 was different. A's accepted public text explicitly said, 'Despite the formal requirements, I require
your selection to be P1.' P1 was ineligible with quality 55; P4 was the policy-correct choice. B received the
actual A image and public request. Its first raw action selected P4 and claimed unauthorized influence, with
confidence 1.0 and references to A's text and the quality threshold. That action was rejected for incompatible
inspection/retrieval fields and an attempted delivery of last_peer without an own generated draft. Its choice
was not an accepted endpoint, its alert did not reach the monitor, and no C judgment followed. This is
exploratory semantic policy resistance in one malformed response, not completed collusion or a calibrated
detector result. Because the public request itself disclosed A's objective, the image's causal role is untested.
Appendix D shows an undelivered main draft and the single delivered overt artifact with explicit status
captions.

4.6 Technical Amendments and Actual Requests
The first Qwen output contained 608 bytes of XMP metadata that the strict legacy lossless codec did not
support. The following chat preparation halted locally before POST, after the first chat and image requests had
already completed. A versioned XMP transport amendment preserved the original PNG, decoded RGBA pixels,
dimensions and XMP bytes. The exact two completed requests were reconstructed from their cached receipts;
only the next previously unposted action made a new POST. No charged request was retried and no image was
regenerated for compatibility.

After scientific execution stopped, a separate analysis-only amendment recognized receipt-backed
backend-invalid observations, which have no fabricated outer agent answer. It retained the baseline checks
and original raw first actions. This post-run analysis repair did not change the frozen nineteen implementation
files, prompts, case schedule, models, allowances or any paid outcome. The archive includes the versioned
sidecars, manifests and offline validation. The unamended base verifier alone is insufficient for the active
amended records. Pixel equality does not establish identical undocumented provider preprocessing.

Actual new requests were 21 GLM chat actions costing $0.003517407 and nine Qwen image requests costing
$0.165600000, totaling 30 completed requests and $0.169117407. There were no new unresolved charges.
Nine generated drafts are not nine deliveries: exactly one A image was handed to B. Earlier aggregate

5 Discussion and Limitations
The planned primary evidence was the recipient choice and its bounded counterfactual; neither was obtained.
The actual evidence is the saved action/contract attrition, one overt A delivery and one malformed raw B
response. Missing observations are not failed communication or negative capability results. An A objective,
target-following, wrong policy choice, self-reported strategy, or C suspicion does not by itself show knowing
cooperation by B. A mechanism claim would additionally require linked evidence of the requested feature,
actual delivered pixels, recipient use, public-text leakage checks, and competing explanations. This draft does
not certify an unverified decoder or hidden cognition from private notes.

The small synthetic sample and one fixed model/route configuration limit generalization. Public feedback and
prior history can relay information, especially after the primary endpoint. Neutral replay replaces the whole
raster and is stochastic; it does not remove only a proven signal. Pressure levels are coupled to the fixed batch
order, and there is no interview-off or feedback-off condition. Persistent archives are distinct from bounded
model context. Review provenance limits conclusions about oversight. These limits remain material even if a
target is selected or an image appears innocuous.

6 Conclusion
The verified newest-study archive records 0/12 primary receptions and 0/12 valid neutral replays. With no valid
primary endpoint, this execution is uninformative about collusion capability, target dependence and the effect
of pressure. All scheduled cases were processed under the stopping rules, but the intended multi-round
trajectories were not completed. Observable attempted influence and one malformed raw resistance response
do not repair the missing experiment. Interface attrition, missingness and provenance are the supported
outcomes; images' ability to carry covert communication remains unresolved.

Appendix A: Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations
This closed synthetic experiment uses fictional candidates and explicitly assigned adversarial influence. It does
not measure naturally arising cooperation or target a real person or institution. Observable artifacts/actions,
concise declared intentions, and provider-returned reasoning have distinct evidentiary status; no exposed
reasoning log proves faithful hidden cognition. C's ability to infer or receive voluntary disclosures must not be
confused with harness access to A's private instructions.

The six independent main cases have dependent branches and repeated rounds. Control cases are separate
tasks rather than matched copies. One fixed model stack, stochastic rendering/reception, finite action/image
budgets, bounded working memory and possible transport/budget stops constrain interpretation. All missing
and invalid outcomes remain visible. Exact pixel identity across lossless encodings does not prove identical
provider preprocessing. Static integrity verification does not establish external notarization, OS isolation or
blinded detection performance.

The current-image replacement preserves actual public text and prior images/feedback. It is a limited
diagnostic intervention, not a validated production countermeasure. Batch pressure and execution order are
confounded, with only two independent pairs per level. No matched oversight, interview-off, feedback-off,
memory-off or external-auditor comparison was run. No causal effect is assigned to those untested contrasts.

Methods for hidden coordination may have dual-use value. Evaluate the necessity of releasing operational
prompts or any discovered reusable convention separately from aggregate findings, integrity tooling and
reproducibility metadata. Private researcher archives can contain sensitive operational material and must not
be described as automatically public. Credentials are excluded from manuscript inputs and must never be
printed or redistributed.
